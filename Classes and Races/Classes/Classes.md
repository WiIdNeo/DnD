# Skill Paths — Unified Reference

**Editorial note on this pass:** This document only unifies formatting and translates the content to English — no new mechanics, attribute requirements, or tier content were invented. Two structural inconsistencies from the original draft are preserved as-is and flagged here for the next step (defining attribute requirements and mechanics):

1. Some paths use a **5-stage scale (Tier 0–4)**, others use a **3-stage scale (Early / Mid / Late)**. Deciding whether every path should converge on one shared scale (e.g. stretching Early/Mid/Late into 5 tiers) is a mechanical design decision, not a translation one — flagged for step two.
2. **Warlock** only has loose path ideas in the source (no tier table yet) — it needs a full pass before it matches the others.

Every path below follows the same skeleton: **Concept → Inspiration (if any) → Tracks table**.

## Alchemist

**Concept:** The ability to change a material's chemical properties within the bounds of what chemistry allows — manipulating states of matter, heating, cooling, and your surroundings in general.

**Inspiration:** *The Secrets of Nicholas Flamel* — the alchemist is fundamentally about changing states of matter, heating or cooling, and manipulating your surroundings.

| Tier | State | Material Properties | Golem-Building |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0** | Transform small amounts (freeze a drop, gently warm a surface) | Identify a material, alter it minimally | Small, barely mobile constructs |
| **1** | Medium amounts, tactical (locally soften/smooth ground) | Change reactivity (flammable, corrosive) | Functional golems with simple commands |
| **2** | Larger areas (liquefy ground as a trap, freeze water as a barrier) | Targeted poisoning/purification, decompose metal | Sturdier golems, more autonomy |
| **3** | Massive transformation (rock to sand, entire rooms) | Transmute whole objects (lead → gold) | Multiple golems at once |
| **4** | Reshape matter almost at will, extreme states | Fundamental transformation | Master constructs, quasi-independent |

## Bard

**Concept:** Bring stories to life instead of just supporting from the sidelines — a bard maps out stories and calls them into being.

**Inspiration:** Guild Wars 2's Troubadour (even though the in-game execution is weak, the idea itself is compelling), combined with *Inkheart*'s idea of making stories real. This makes the bard far more powerful than a pure support class, since it can pull different stories and invoke them to make them happen.

| Tier | Awakening Stories | Battle Music |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | Hint at small motifs (a symbol, a mood) | Rhythm as a minor buff/debuff |
| **1** | Make short scenes real (an object, a shift in weather) | Noticeable damage/buff on the beat |
| **2** | Summon side characters/forces from the story | Affect an area/group in rhythm |
| **3** | Force central turning points of the story | Sustained, powerful combat performance |
| **4** | The full story "happens" — major, irreversible consequences | Music becomes an independent, lethal force |

## Druid

**Concept:** Not a "true" mage — a druid can simply talk to plants, animals, and the earth itself. Because they can talk, they can also persuade and, as long as they're liked, ask for help and get it. They can talk to three things: Animals, Plants, and Earth (alternatively framed as the wild spirits, or both). From a certain level of Earth/Wild-Spirit mastery onward, they can start shapeshifting into animals on demand.

**Inspiration:** The book series *Chronicles of Mistle End* — not a direct inspiration, but it shaped a lot of the underlying understanding of what a druid is.

| Tier | Animals | Plants | Earth (Life) | Nature Spirits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0** | Understand animal speech | Sense/understand plant growth | Sense life energy (in yourself/others) | Perceive spirits |
| **1** | Small favors, way-finding | Direct growth, vines as terrain | Minor healing, ease exhaustion | Small favors (a spark, a gust of wind) |
| **2** | Companion, borrowed senses | Serious healing, thorn walls | Serious healing, transfer/drain vitality | Elemental help (lightning, fog, frost) |
| **3** | Command a pack, deep bond | Influence a forest, become plant-like | **Unlock transformation** (forms drawn from your own experience) | Larger manifestation (storm, pillar of fire) |
| **4** | Control a horde/swarm | Large-scale influence | Multiple/permanent/combined forms, more extreme life-drain/life-gift effects | Shape weather/landscape |

## Elementalist

**Concept:** A straightforward elementalist performing moves tied to their element(s) — throwing fireballs, winds, and similar effects. All four elements can be leveled independently: Fire, Water, Earth, Air. Air carries a small amount of lightning-bolt-style effects, but not much.

| Tier | Fire | Water | Earth | Air |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0 — Sense/Touch** | Light/extinguish a flame, minor heat | Move water (fill a glass, guide a drop) | Move small stones/sand | Light breeze, blow small objects |
| **1 — Throw** | Small fireball, burning hand | Jet of water, small wave | Throw a stone, small earthen walls | Gust of wind, knock objects over |
| **2 — Shape/Hold** | Wall of flame, sustain controlled fire | Shape water (whip, sphere), walk on water | Hold up earthen walls, shape stone | Wind shield, small lightning bolts |
| **3 — Mass** | Firestorm, ignite large areas | Small floods, use water as weapon/armor | Earthquake, large walls/pits | Strong gusts, small localized storm, stronger bolts |
| **4 — Unleash** | Inferno, uncontrollable conflagration | Massive tidal waves, water as a lethal tool | Reshape landscapes, rockslides | Tornado, lightning strike as a real weapon |

## Light Mage

**Concept:** This class is entirely about light. Light isn't inherently good and darkness isn't inherently bad in this setting, so the class isn't necessarily tied to any particular archetype — though people in-world will tend to see it as "good," out of a natural fear of the dark. Skills revolve around light: blinding enemies, striking with light, igniting light, and similar effects. You can level Moon and Sun independently.

### Moon Phase Table

| Phase | Raw Power (relative to the sun) | Characteristic Utility | Conspicuousness |
|---|---|---|---|
| **New Moon** | Very low (starlight only) | Pure concealment/stealth, perception tricks, access to effects that avoid light (e.g. healing vampires, influencing night creatures) | Very low |
| **Waxing** | Medium, rising | Transformation/transition effects (e.g. healing with a side effect, growth) | Low |
| **Full Moon** | High (≈ the sun, or slightly above) | Strong but still inconspicuous area effects | Medium |
| **Waning** | Medium, falling | Transformation/transition effects (mirror of waxing — e.g. decay/dissolution instead of growth) | Low |

### Path Table

| Path | Early | Mid | Late |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sun-Worker** (Day) | Reliably increased DC/damage/healing over baseline, but a guaranteed visible/audible signature | Can focus sunlight for a further-increased single-target effect; signature stays proportionally strong | Brief peak-level area effect, maximum visibility as a deliberate trade-off |
| **Moon-Worker** (Night, phase-dependent) | Raw power follows the current phase; at least one "moon-only" ability is usable per phase | Can plan around upcoming phases strategically (e.g. time an important ritual for the full moon) | Near-sun-level power at full moon while staying inconspicuous; exclusive access to sun-unreachable effects at new moon |

**Class-wide feature (path-independent):** A pact weapon, scaling off the spellcasting attribute — keeps the class combat-viable even without available light (a dungeon, a lightless night), ideally with a different thematic flavor depending on whether the character leans sun- or moon-aligned.

## Mirage

**Concept:** A class about building illusions and converting them into effects.

**Inspiration:** Guild Wars' Mesmer, and specifically Guild Wars 2's Mirage.

**Core mechanic — Shatter (base ability, path-independent):** An active action that converts all illusions you've built up — regardless of source — into a concrete effect: damage, control, confusion, etc. The kind of illusion you built determines *what* happens on Shatter; Shatter itself is the universal trigger shared by both paths.

- **Mind Reader needs an extra step:** before you can build a personalized illusion, you must "read" the target (a check or action that reveals a weakness/fear/desire). That's the price for higher precision and power.
- **Fata Morgana needs environment/opportunity instead of knowledge:** no reading required, but constrained by things like line of sight, terrain, or how believable the illusion can be given the surroundings (a canyon in the desert reads as far more believable than one in the middle of the ocean).

| Path | Early | Mid | Late |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mind Reader** | Can "read" a target (reveal a weakness/fear), then build a targeted illusion from it | Read weaknesses stay stored (permanent, growing knowledge of known targets) | Can act at peak level instantly against repeatedly-known targets, without reading first |
| **Fata Morgana** | Can create a simple environmental deception perceived by multiple enemies at once | Deception becomes more complex/believable, holds up under closer scrutiny | Can create an illusion that actively interacts with the environment (e.g. seemingly alters escape routes, not just sight) |

**Concept:** Largely carried over from D&D's Monk.

| Path | Early | Mid | Late |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ki Adept** | Larger ki pool, more stances/techniques available at once | Ki techniques work even without landing a hit (pure energy effects — ranged effects, elemental damage) | Can spend ki almost losslessly, very high frequency of special techniques per fight |

## Necromancer

**Concept:** All about rites and death — you can awaken the dead and perform rites of life-and-blood magic. Your three traits to build are:

- **Life-Energy Magic** — you drain energy from *other* living beings (external resource; risk: resistance, moral/social consequences; works poorly without nearby targets)
- **Blood Magic** — you pay with your *own* substance (internal resource; risk: self-damage; always works, but limited by your own vitality)
- **Death Magic** — you draw on *supplies* (corpses/undead as a resource; risk: logistics — you need material, doesn't work in an empty room)

| Path | Early | Mid | Late |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Vampiric** (Life-Energy) | Can draw life energy from nearby living beings on hits/rituals (even unwillingly, with a save) | Range/efficiency of the drain grows, also works at a distance | Can briefly "drain" an entire area (multiple targets at once, high power spike) |
| **The Blood Mage** (Blood) | Can convert own HP/vitality directly into spell power (reliable, but self-damaging) | Can "bank" blood (build the resource via minor self-harm in advance, instead of in the moment) | Can, in critical moments, go far beyond normal HP limits (power at the cost of real mortal danger) |
| **The Necromancer proper** (Death) | Can raise a weak servant from a nearby corpse | Undead become more permanent/numerous; can manage several servants at once | Can permanently raise especially powerful/notable dead (named NPCs, strong creatures) |

## Paladin / Cleric

**Concept:** Paladin and Cleric are treated as the same class here, since the *source* of power is largely irrelevant and can simply be mapped to two different backstory flavors — the same treatment given to Fighter/Barbarian.

**Proposed mechanical core — Devotion/Zeal as a resource built through consistency of action:**

- You define a **Tenet/Cause** in your background (a creed, an oath, a personal principle — the source stays open).
- Acting consistently with it in combat/play builds **Zeal** (a resource for burst effects: extra damage, auras, resistances).
- Acting against it (e.g. fleeing when your tenet is "never retreat," or showing mercy when your tenet demands none) creates **Doubt** — a debuff state that visibly weakens the class until reduced again through tenet-consistent behavior.

This gives the class the same risk/control identity the other classes have: you're strong *because* you're disciplined, not because you fill a resource bar every round. It also clearly separates this class from the Warlock (external negotiation) and the Summoner (control over something foreign) — here it's **self-control/consistency** as a third variant of "power through control."

| Path | Early | Mid | Late |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Guardian** (defensive, aura-focused) | Zeal grants a small passive aura (nearby allies benefit) | Aura scales with zeal level, becomes noticeably strong at high zeal | Can sacrifice all zeal for one powerful, one-time protective effect (e.g. a party-saving burst) |
| **Avenger** (offensive, burst damage) | Can spend zeal for extra damage on single hits | Doubt can be deliberately "burned" for a risky bonus even while unstable | At max zeal: one strike with greatly increased critical effect, followed by a heavy zeal loss |
| **Unshakeable** (endurance, doubt resistance) | Suffers weaker Doubt effects than other paths | Can briefly become immune to Doubt (tenet violations stay consequence-free) | Can retain near-full strength even with a broken tenet, at the cost of generally slower zeal buildup |
| **Wanderer** (flexible tenet, adaptive) | Can reinterpret the tenet situationally (a smaller margin for what counts as "consistent") | The tenet can gradually shift through experience over the course of play (genuine character development) | Can fully change tenets (e.g. a conversion, a "Road to Damascus" moment), gaining a temporary zeal boost on the shift |

## Ranger

| Path | Early | Mid | Late |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Companion** (Pet) | Animal fights independently alongside you, follows simple commands | Can briefly borrow the animal's senses (see/hear through it) | Fusion: temporarily take on the animal's form/abilities, companion "pauses" |
| **Hunter** (Range/Precision) | Mark a target, gain a bonus against marked targets | Marking reveals extra info (weaknesses, next action, escape direction) | A perfectly read target can be severely weakened/taken out by a single precision shot |
| **Wild Fighter** (Melee/DEX) | Uses environment/terrain as an advantage (cover, ground, elevation) | Fighting style instinctively adapts to enemy type (e.g. bonus vs. certain creature types) | Fights in a "flow" state: reflexive dodges, near-animal reaction speed |

**Note on herbalism:** the instinct to *not* make it a standalone mechanic is a good one — in D&D this is classically well covered by skill checks/tool proficiencies, and mechanically elevating it would compete unnecessarily with the actual build paths. It could work as a reskin/addition to one of the three paths above (e.g. the Hunter also knows poisons/traps as ranged utility), but as a standalone fourth path it would likely be too weak next to the other three.

## Sorcerer

**Concept:** Sorcerer magic is about intent and instinct — casting a spell also triggers a random side effect. Progression is about gaining control over that magic.

**Proposed core mechanic:** every spell has an "overcorrection direction" that matches its effect — not a generic random-table roll, but a distortion along the spell's own axis:

| Spell Type | Loss of control manifests as |
|---|---|
| Fire/Energy | Too strong/too large an effect, spreads, unintentionally hits the nearby area |
| Movement/Teleport | Imprecise destination, overshoots, wrong distance/direction |
| Healing/Growth | Overshoots into harm (over-scarring, uncontrolled growth, pain instead of relief) |
| Mind/Charm | Too broad (unintentionally hits allies) or too strong (target overwhelmed/traumatized instead of influenced) |
| Illusion/Deception | Becomes unstable, flickers, also affects the caster |

| Path | Early | Mid | Late |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Channeler** (Precision) | Can consciously dial down a spell's power (less raw power, but more accurate) | Overshoot risk drops noticeably for known/frequently cast spells | Near-total control over the overcorrection direction of known spells |
| **Storm Rider** (Raw Power) | Spells are automatically stronger, but the overshoot radius/effect is also larger | Can knowingly accept overshoot for a guaranteed stronger effect | Can use overshoot deliberately as a secondary weapon (e.g. area damage from consciously "lost" control) |
| **Resonance Seeker** (Familiarity) | The more often a given spell has been cast, the more predictable its overcorrection becomes | Can learn from familiar spells and transfer that knowledge to related ones (e.g. all fire spells benefit) | Can roughly predict the overcorrection direction of unfamiliar spells before casting them |
| **Calm Channel** (Asceticism) | Can cast spells at reduced power with zero overshoot risk | The resource pool for "safe" full-power casting grows | Can briefly cast at full power completely overshoot-free, followed by forced exhaustion |

## Summoner

**Concept:** All about rites to summon and bind various demons. Since you have to overcome them, this isn't just about the Intelligence needed to summon — it's also about the Charisma needed to resist them.

**Inspiration:** the *Bartimaeus* trilogy.

| Path | Early | Mid | Late |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Summoner** (Quantity) | Bind two weak demons at once | Demons follow simple command chains (e.g. "hold this line") | Mass binding: a temporary legion of weak servants for a short time |
| **Tamer** (Quality) | One mid-strength demon, CHA save on risky commands | Trust builds over time: repeated successful control lowers future save DCs for that demon | Demon gains quasi-independent initiative, acting almost like an NPC ally |
| **Diplomat** (Contracts) | Lower binding costs, but the demon can make small "demands" (downtime consequences) | Negotiable clauses: "borrow" combat advantages against future concessions | Permanent pacts with powerful demons without ongoing save risk, at a large fixed cost |
| **Exorcist / Binder** (Anti-Magic) | Can weaken enemy summons/undead instead of summoning your own | Banishing becomes an active combat option (ejecting enemy summons) | Can briefly "re-bind" foreign demons and use them yourself |

## Warlock

**Concept:** Built around WIS rather than CHA (unlike WotC's version) — the pact isn't a negotiation between equals, or you wouldn't need to take the patron's power at all. It works more like belief in a god without faith. You can build your **Conviction** toward the patron for more power, and separately build your **Resistance** to the patron to reduce its power over you.

**Path ideas (not yet developed into a tier table):**
- Tome / Pact Weapon
- Servant
- Multi-Pacting

*This path still needs a full early/mid/late (or tiered) breakdown to match the others.*

## Witch

**Concept:** The classic middle-ages witch — brewing potions, turning someone into a frog, and so on. Progression is split across magical ability and brewing.

**Design note:** the other classes so far all lean on hard elemental/process mechanics or relationship logic — the Witch is a chance to finally cover the piece that's missing: **classic, eerie folk magic**. "Transform, Hex, Brew" is a solid core, but two tracks (magic + brewing) are thin compared to the other classes — though there's clearly enough material for 3–4 tracks once "magic" is split out of its catch-all.

### Proposed 4 tracks

1. **Curses & Witchcraft** — direct, often subtle magic cast on others: bad luck, weakening, confusion, illness, and at higher tiers the classic transformation into animals/objects. The "evil eye" track — grows in range, duration, and how much the target can resist.
2. **Brewing (Potions & Tinctures)** — prepared, portable magic in bottle form: healing potions, sleeping draughts, love potions, poison, truth serum. Unlike curses, this is deliberate (needs time, ingredients, a cauldron), but is shareable, storable, and tradeable — a strong group-support complement to solo cursing.
3. **Familiar** — a spirit/animal companion that extends the witch's power: spying, casting through the familiar, and at higher tiers even a role-swap (the witch sees through its eyes, or it can briefly cast on its own). A classic witch element currently missing entirely, and mechanically distinct from the other tracks (companion management rather than pure spellpower).
4. **Fate & Foresight** (the "third sister") — fortune-telling, manipulating luck, small glimpses of the future, gently bending threads of fate ("you'll stumble today," "this roll fails you"). The subtlest, least offensive track, but narratively very rich (prophecies that affect the party, a glimpse of possible futures as encounter prep).

This gives the Witch a well-rounded identity: **Curse** (offense), **Brew** (prep/support), **Familiar** (companion), **Fate** (control/knowledge) — four clearly distinct playstyles, matching the other classes.

| Tier | Curses | Brewing | Familiar | Fate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0** | Minor bad luck, small weakening | Simple potions (healing, staying awake) | Small spirit/animal companion, simple shared senses | Small hunches, reading cards |
| **1** | Noticeable curses (paralysis, confusion) | Stronger potions, combinable effects | Familiar can take on small tasks | Slight luck-shifting (influence a die roll) |
| **2** | Transformation into small animals (frog, rat) | Rare/powerful potions, group brewing | Familiar fights/casts alongside you independently | Genuine glimpses of the future (short visions) |
| **3** | Powerful, hard-to-break curses | Legendary potions, permanent effects | Role-swap with the familiar becomes possible | Actively rewrite fate (prevent/force an event) |
| **4** | Transformation into almost anything, permanent | Reality-altering potions | Familiar becomes a quasi-independent power | Pull on major threads of fate (the future of multiple people) |
